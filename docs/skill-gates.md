# Skill Gates

## Gate A: row operations, elimination, and LU

Pass when two systems are solved by elimination and each pivot step is explained as a row operation. The solution should connect elimination to an upper-triangular form and, for a square example, to \(A=LU\).

## Gate B: matrix multiplication, inverses, and transposes

Pass when products, inverses, and transposes are computed in small examples and explained through both row/column action and composition. Include at least one check that \(AA^{-1}=I\).

## Gate C: vector spaces and subspaces

Pass when 10 candidate subsets are classified with clear use of closure under addition, closure under scalar multiplication, and presence or absence of the zero vector.

## Gate D: column space, nullspace, and solvability

Pass when three matrices are analyzed by finding \(C(A)\), \(N(A)\), and the condition on \(b\) for \(Ax=b\) to be solvable.

## Gate E: RREF, rank, free variables, and special solutions

Pass when two rectangular matrices are reduced to \(R\), pivot and free variables are identified, rank is stated, and a basis for \(N(A)\) is written from special solutions.

## Gate F: complete solutions of \(Ax=b\)

Pass when the four rank cases are classified and a complete solution is written as \(x=x_p+x_n\) whenever the system is consistent.

## Gate G: independence, span, basis, and dimension

Pass when five vector sets are classified for independence, span, basis, and dimension using rank and nullspace evidence.

## Gate H: four fundamental subspaces

Pass when, for two matrices, bases and dimensions are computed for \(C(A)\), \(N(A)\), \(C(A^T)\), and \(N(A^T)\), with the dimension counts checked against rank-nullity.

## Gate I: orthogonality, projections, least squares, and \(QR\)

Pass when a projection problem and a least-squares problem are solved, with the normal equation \(A^TA\hat{x}=A^Tb\) and one \(QR\)-based view explained.

## Gate J: determinants

Pass when determinants are computed by row operations and cofactors, and determinant meaning is explained as signed volume scaling.

## Gate K: eigenvalues, diagonalization, and applications

Pass when eigenvalues and eigenvectors are found, diagonalization is performed when possible, and \(A^k\) or \(e^{At}\) is used in an application.

## Gate L: symmetric and positive definite matrices

Pass when positive definiteness is checked through pivots, eigenvalues, and quadratic forms, and the geometric meaning of \(x^TAx>0\) is explained.

## Gate M: complex matrices and FFT

Pass when complex inner products are handled correctly and one small Fourier matrix example is computed.

## Gate N: similar matrices and Jordan form

Pass when similarity invariants are identified and the reason for Jordan form is explained in a non-diagonalizable example.

## Gate O: SVD, change of basis, and image compression

Pass when an SVD is computed for a small matrix and a rank-\(k\) approximation is explained.

## Gate P: linear transformations and pseudoinverse

Pass when a matrix is built from a linear transformation and one least-squares or minimum-norm problem is solved using \(A^+\).
